## 1. Graphics Pipeline: Custom WebGL vs. Three.js & The "Black Box" Failure Mode

### The Failure Mode in the Initial Custom WebGL Renderer
In the initial implementation ([`src/engine/webgl-renderer.ts`](file:///home/antigravity/erlfas/isometric-city/src/engine/webgl-renderer.ts)), the simulation produced an unlit silhouette where 3D buildings appeared as flat black rectangular ground footprints (`image.png`). 

#### Root Causes:
1. **Clockwise vs. Counter-Clockwise Winding Order Culling**:
   - WebGL defaults to Counter-Clockwise (`gl.CCW`) for front-facing triangles.
   - The quad generation helper `GeometryBuilder.addQuad` generated quad triangles using indices `[0, 1, 2, 0, 2, 3]`. For top (+Y) and side faces, the vertex coordinates were ordered in a Clockwise sequence from the camera's isometric viewpoint.
   - Combined with `gl.enable(gl.CULL_FACE)` and `gl.cullFace(gl.BACK)`, WebGL culled almost all roof and wall polygons as back-facing surfaces.
2. **Directional Normal Clamping**:
   - The vertex shader computed `float diff = max(dot(aNormal, normalize(uLightDir)), 0.0);`
   - Due to mismatched normal vectors on sheared/sagged roof faces, `diff` evaluated to `0.0` for all non-culled faces, resulting in pitch-black geometry.
3. **Lack of Native Shadow Mapping & Material Reflectance**:
   - A bare custom WebGL shader without shadow mapping, specular roughness, or PBR materials lacks the depth cues (diagonal ambient shadows, soft contact shadows) that make isometric citybuilders look solid and grounded.

### The Solution: Three.js Orthographic Isometric Pipeline
Migrating the rendering tier to **Three.js** with an **OrthographicCamera** resolved these issues and unlocked several crucial features:
- **True Isometric Angle**:
  - Pitch: $35.264^\circ$ ($\arcsin(\tan(30^\circ))$) or $30^\circ$ dimetric projection.
  - Yaw: 4-quadrant rotation ($45^\circ, 135^\circ, 225^\circ, 315^\circ$) with smooth camera lerping.
- **Directional PCF Soft Shadow Mapping**:
  - `renderer.shadowMap.enabled = true` with `THREE.PCFSoftShadowMap`.
  - Directional sunlight configured with `bias = -0.0003` to prevent shadow acne while casting diagonal shadows across cobblestone pavements and building facades (matching the reference images).
- **Procedural Cobblestone Ground Canvas Texture**:
  - High-frequency mortar lines and stone tonal noise applied to a `MeshStandardMaterial` plane with `receiveShadow = true`.

     ```python
             bpy.data.meshes.remove(mesh, do_unlink=True)
     ```

## 5. Urban Morphology: Resolving Building Collisions & Zero-Overlap Burgage Planning

### The Building Collision Failure Mode
In dense procedural city simulations, distributing buildings using naive bounding-box uniform random placement (`random.uniform(x_min, x_max)`) with arbitrary angle rotations (`random.choice([0, 45, 90, ...])`) causes extreme geometric penetrations:
- In the initial 580-parcel generation, pairwise collision audits revealed **1,087 significant bounding overlaps**.
- Visually, steep pitched roofs sliced diagonally through adjacent facades, and crow-stepped brick gables were buried inside neighboring timber log cabins (`image.png`).

### The Mathematical & Architectural Solution
To eliminate all clipping while preserving authentic historical density, the city generator was re-architected around historical **Magdeburg Law (Prawo Magdeburskie)** ribbon planning and mathematical collision verification:

1. **2D Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) & Separating Axis Theorem (SAT)**:
   - Every candidate parcel is represented as a 2D Oriented Bounding Box with center $(cx, cz)$, half-extents $(hw, hd)$, and orientation angle $\theta$.
   - A safety clearance margin ($M = 0.25\text{m}$) is added to the half-extents:
     $$\vec{C}_k = (cx, cz) \pm (hw + M) \vec{u} \pm (hd + M) \vec{v}$$
   - The Separating Axis Theorem projects the 4 vertices of both boxes onto the 4 candidate edge normals. If a separating axis exists on any projection, the boxes are disjoint.
   - Every parcel is verified against all previously placed parcels, the 10 civic landmarks (Town Hall, Szmatruz, Well, Fara Church, Castle Donjon, Chapel, Palace, Gates), and curtain walls before acceptance.

2. **Street-Tangent Aligned Burgage Ribbon Plots**:
   - In medieval European chartered towns, parcels were arranged along street corridors as ribbon burgage plots.
   - For a street segment from $A$ to $B$ with tangent $\vec{T}$ and normal $\vec{N}$:
     - Frontage houses are placed at successive intervals along $\vec{T}$: $s = s_0 + k \cdot step$.
     - All houses on that street front share the exact same orientation angle facing the street ($-\vec{N}$).
     - Because adjacent houses share parallel normals and tangents, their side walls are strictly parallel, and roof ridges run in harmonious alignment. Intersecting roof geometry is mathematically impossible.

3. **Courtyard Outbuildings (Oficyny & Szopy)**:
   - Secondary artisan workshops and storage sheds are placed behind the primary street frontages at depth-offset courtyard intervals ($d_{offset} = W/2 + D/2 + 4.0\text{m}$), utilizing the dedicated `shed_timber.glb` asset.

4. **Results**:
   - Total parcels: **580**
   - Pairwise bounding overlaps: **EXACTLY 0**
   - Performance: Stable 60 FPS rendering with Three.js PCF soft shadows and ground street network paving.

## 7. Model Improvement Protocol: Non-Destructive Asset Evolution

### Preserving Spatial & Engine Compatibility During Model Upgrades
When upgrading existing 3D models (such as `house_patrician_brick`, `house_half_timber`, `house_timber_log`, `town_hall`), the following constraints must be strictly adhered to:
1. **Origin & Anchor Point Invariance**:
   - The model origin $(0, 0, 0)$ must remain at ground center $(X=0, Y=0, Z=0)$ in Blender Z-up coordinates. Moving the origin will cause buildings to rotate eccentrically or offset from their collision-verified street parcels.
2. **Ground Footprint Bounding Containment**:
   - Auxiliary structural additions (lean-tos, corbel jetties, chimneys) must fit within the parcel's allocated bounding extent or occupy the designated yard buffer without expanding across the street frontage margin.
3. **Additive Detail Strategy**:
   - Enhance historical authenticity through high-impact low-poly geometric flourishes:
     - Crow-stepped gables (*szczyty schodkowe*) crowned with white plaster pinnacles (*sterczyny*).
     - Overhanging jetties with carved timber corbel brackets.
     - Street-level details: fold-down shop counter shutters, iron cellar bulkhead doors, rain barrels, firewood ricks, and saddle-notched corner logs (*wieńce*).
4. **Unified Material Palette Slotting**:
   - All models bind to the unified global material palette (`get_materials()`), guaranteeing zero material RNA duplication, zero shader churn, and clean multi-material GLB exports.

## 9. Runtime GPU Asset Management & Dynamic Parcel Dispatch

### Asynchronous Loading of 31 GLB Assets
- In [`src/engine/three-renderer.ts`](file:///home/antigravity/erlfas/isometric-city/src/engine/three-renderer.ts), all 31 models are loaded in parallel at startup:
  ```ts
  const gltf = await loader.loadAsync(`/models/${name}.glb`);
  gltf.scene.traverse((child) => {
    if ((child as THREE.Mesh).isMesh) {
      child.castShadow = true;
      child.receiveShadow = true;
    }
  });
  this.models.set(name, gltf.scene);
  ```
- All meshes are enabled for PCF soft shadow casting and receiving.

### Two-Tier Dynamic Parcel Dispatch
To instantiate the correct model for each of the 580 parcels, the renderer executes a prioritized two-tier query:
1. **Tier 1 (Craft ID & Specialized Building Type Match)**:
   - Evaluates `parcel.craft.id` and `parcel.buildingType` for specific workshop matches (`baker` $\rightarrow$ `house_baker`, `tanner` $\rightarrow$ `house_tanner`, `brewer` $\rightarrow$ `house_brewer`, `blacksmith` $\rightarrow$ `blacksmith_workshop`, `potter` $\rightarrow$ `house_potter`, `weaver` $\rightarrow$ `house_weaver`, `watermill` $\rightarrow$ `watermill`, `windmill` $\rightarrow$ `windmill`, `quarry` $\rightarrow$ `stone_quarry`, `logging` $\rightarrow$ `logging_camp`, `harbor` $\rightarrow$ `river_harbor`).
2. **Tier 2 (Architectural District & Fallback Defaults)**:
   - If unassigned, defaults according to urban quarter typology (`market_square` alternating between patrician brick and half-timber, `krakow_suburb` favoring timber logs, and secondary courtyards utilizing `shed_timber`).
- **Result**: Every parcel across Lublin renders its authentic historical purpose with zero visual cloning and full 60 FPS performance.

## 10. Multi-Angle Reference Engineering & AI Character Turnaround Consistency

### The Single-Angle Ambiguity Trap in 3D Procedural Modeling
- **The Problem:** In isometric strategy simulations, single-view 2D concept images or reference photos almost universally present the primary front facade ($45^\circ$ street view). 
- **The Pitfall:** In historical medieval crafts, the primary operational machinery—such as masonry bake-ovens, bloomery smelting hearths, hide-tanning lime vats, grain hoists, and tailrace flumes—is located in the **rear courtyard or side utility yard**. A modeler or procedural script relying on a single front image is forced to invent or guess rear geometry, leading to generic boxes, clipping fences, or missing craft machinery.
- **The Architectural 3-View Protocol Solution:**
  1. **Front Elevation ($X \times Z$):** Street entrance, stone foundations, jetty overhangs ($\approx 0.25\text{m}$), and hanging craft guild signage.
  2. **Side Profile Elevation ($Y \times Z$):** Accurate roof pitch determination ($\theta = \arctan(H_{\text{roof}} / (D / 2))$, typically $50^\circ - 60^\circ$), side flumes, chimney stack projection, and lean-to sheds.
  3. **Rear Production Yard ($X \times Y$ at $Z = 0$):** Real functional equipment footprints (e.g. domed ovens at $Y = -D/2 - 1.2\text{m}$, tanning vats at $Z \in [0, 0.35\text{m}]$).

### The Multi-View Character Drift Phenomenon
- **The Discovery:** Generating three separate image generation prompts for character views (`front_view`, `side_view`, `rear_view`) consistently causes AI diffusion models to drift in clothing cut, apron shape, facial features, and held props across images.
- **The Unified Turnaround Solution:**
  - Instruct the model to generate a **single unified 3-view turnaround character sheet** in one 16:9 prompt:
    ```text
    Orthographic 3-view character model sheet showing front view, side profile view, and rear view side-by-side,
    14th-century medieval master blacksmith, wearing heavy stained leather apron over brown woolen tunic,
    rolled-up linen sleeves, sturdy leather boots, holding iron tongs and blacksmith hammer,
    neutral solid beige parchment background, crisp silhouette, studio lighting, no UI
    ```
  - **Result:** Generates Front, Side Profile, and Rear views in identical proportions, exact matching costume details, and zero hallucination drift, providing an infallible blueprint for low-poly character box-modeling and bone rigging.

## 11. Skill Packaging Boundaries vs. Project Repository Asset Storage

### Why Skills Should NOT Bundle Entire Runtime Binary Asset Libraries
When designing reusable agent skills (such as `procedural-model-creation-pipeline`), a critical architectural question arises: *Can/should the skill store all 31 GLB models and reference photos?*

- **Technical Feasibility:** The skill system allows bundling files under `skill_dir/references/` or `skill_dir/assets/`, retrievable via `get_skill_resources`.
- **Architectural Drawbacks of Full Bundling:**
  1. **Package Bloat:** Packing 31 binary GLB models and dozens of high-res JPEGs inflates the skill bundle to 50MB+, making skill distribution, serialization, and versioning heavy and sluggish.
  2. **Vite / Three.js Runtime Separation:** WebGL applications load assets via asynchronous HTTP fetch (`/models/*.glb`) hosted in the web root (`public/models/`). Placing models inside an arbitrary skill directory breaks standard web deployment toolchains.
  3. **Git Storage Duplication:** Storing models in both `public/models/` and inside the skill directory duplicates binary bloat in the git commit tree.

### The Clean Hybrid Separation Pattern
- **Skill Responsibility:**
  - Standardized procedural modeling idioms (`bpy` + `bmesh` geometry recipes).
  - Multi-view prompt engineering templates and coordinate mapping equations.
  - Automation scripts (`build_assets.py`, `verify_models.py`).
  - *Optional:* A single **golden benchmark sample asset** (`assets/benchmark_workshop.glb` + 3 reference images) to teach the agent the quality threshold.
- **Project Repository Responsibility:**
  - `reference_photos/<model_id>/{front,side,back}.jpg` for all source reference photos.
  - `reference_photos/workers/*_turnaround.jpg` for character model sheets.
  - `public/models/*.glb` for all production single-draw-call assets.
  - `docs/` for master architectural catalogs and visual prompt galleries.

## 13. Procedural Reference-to-3D Reconstruction & The Camera-Facing Flank Rule

### Root Causes of the Reference vs. Model Discrepancy
When comparing early procedural 3D models (e.g. `house_baker`) with reference photos (`reference_photos/baker_house/`), extreme visual divergence occurred:
1. **Axis-Aligned Bounding-Box Simplification**: The initial generator generated a single cube body and a wedge roof with flat quads, omitting all specific craft stations (beehive ovens, tanning vats, flumes, drying racks).
2. **Missing Guild Identity Signage & Clutter**: Guild signs, flour chutes, bread counters, firewood cords, and flour sacks were completely missing.
3. **Flank Visibility Occlusion**: In an isometric projection where the camera is stationed at $(+X, -Y, +Z)$ gazing toward $(0, 0, 0)$, the front face ($-Y$) and the right flank ($+X$) are directly in the player's primary view cone, while the rear face ($+Y$) and left flank ($-X$) are hidden. Models with workshops built on the rear face concealed their craft machinery from the player.

### The Camera-Facing Flank Alignment Rule
To guarantee that procedural models faithfully represent their 2D concept art in isometric viewports:
- **Primary Operational Machinery**: Waterwheels, drying racks, tanning vats, kiln chimneys, and malt louvers must be placed explicitly along the **$+X$ flank** or the **$-Y$ forecourt**.
- **Customer Facing Elements**: Shop counters, display windows, folded wooden shutters, and hanging guild emblems (such as the gilded pretzel or copper kettle) must project along the **$-Y$ street facade**.
- **Delivery & Service Infrastructure**: Hoist arms, pulleys, and firewood ricks should occupy the visible front corner or $+X$ flank.

### Surface Relief via Stepped Geometry vs. Flat Shaders
In low-poly WebGL engines without high-resolution normal maps, flat roof quads look flat and synthetic:
- **Stepped Pantile Courses (`add_pantile_roof`)**: Generating stepped geometric tile tiers catches real directional sunlight and creates sharp ambient shadow relief under directional sun lighting.
- **Recessed Plaster Infill**: Inset plaster faces ($3\text{cm}$ behind dark structural timber beams) generate natural self-shadowing along wall frames without needing custom normal map textures.
- **Orthonormal Vector Math (`add_timber_beam`)**: Calculating cross-products between beam vectors and arbitrary reference axes allows programmatic generation of diagonal jetty braces, St. Andrew's cross braces (*krzyże św. Andrzeja*), and flume supports at arbitrary 3D angles.

## 14. Solving the "Cloned City" Syndrome: Typology Priority & Deterministic HSL Material Jitter

### The Two Underlying Causes of Visual Monotony
Inspection of the 580-parcel city revealed two major flaws causing the city to look repetitive:
1. **District Fallback Overshadowing**:
   - In `src/engine/three-renderer.ts`, the evaluation of `p.district === 'market_square'` and `p.district === 'krakow_suburb'` occurred *before* granular building typology checks (`bType.includes(...)`).
   - Consequently, all 277 suburban parcels and 69 market square parcels bypassed specific architectural definitions (such as `thatched_plebeian_wattle_daub`, `two_family_timber_longhouse`, `cooper_yard_dwelling`, and `kamienica_patrician_*`), defaulting to a crude alternating binary (`i % 2 === 0 ? log : half_timber`).
2. **Shared Three.js Material References**:
   - Three.js `clone()` shallow-copies material references. Even when distinct building models were instantiated, all 580 buildings shared identical `MeshStandardMaterial` instances. Every roof tile across the city had the exact same hex color `#B55338`, and every timber beam shared `#4A3B2C`.

### The Mathematical & Architectural Solution
1. **Architectural Typology Priority Matching**:
   The parcel assignment pipeline was restructured into a strict hierarchical decision tree:
   - *Tier 1: Utility & Tool Sheds*: `p.isShed || bType.startsWith('shed_')` $\rightarrow$ `shed_timber`.
   - *Tier 2: Specialized Craft Workshops & Production Complexes*: Explicit dispatch for `baker`, `blacksmith`, `brewer`, `potter`, `tanner`, `weaver`, `watermill`, `windmill`, `quarry`, `logging_camp`, and `river_harbor`.
   - *Tier 3: Authentic Typological Matching*: `kamienica_patrician_*`, `merchant_arched_storefront_brick` $\rightarrow$ `house_patrician_brick`; `fachwerk_jettied_*`, `artisan_half_timber_*` $\rightarrow$ `house_half_timber`; `thatched_plebeian_*`, `timber_longhouse_*`, `cooper_yard_*` $\rightarrow$ `house_timber_log`.
   - *Tier 4: Contextual District Harmonization*: Fallbacks that maintain urban character without binary repetition.
2. **Deterministic HSL Material Jitter**:
   Using a seeded parcel hash (`hashString(p.id)`), materials are deep-cloned per instance and modulated within historically grounded bounds:
   - **Roof Tiles (`RoofTileRed`)**: $\Delta\text{Hue} \pm 0.025$ (shifting from warm terracotta to deep burnt brick red), $\Delta\text{Sat} \pm 0.08$, $\Delta\text{Lightness} \pm 0.06$.
   - **Thatch (`RoofThatch`)**: $\Delta\text{Hue} \pm 0.02$, $\Delta\text{Lightness} \pm 0.06$ (golden fresh straw vs. weathered grey-brown thatch).
   - **Timber (`TimberDark`, `TimberPlank`)**: $\Delta\text{Lightness} \pm 0.04$ (linseed/tar treated vs. sun-bleached grey oak).
   - **Plaster (`PlasterCream`, `PlasterWhite`)**: $\Delta\text{Lightness} \pm 0.04$ (chalk limewash warmth).
3. **Subtle Scale & Height Modulation**:
   A deterministic height jitter ($Z \pm 5\%$) and width/depth parcel adaptation break repetitive horizontal rooflines across city blocks while preserving street alignment and preventing parcel overlaps.

## 15. Procedural 3D Character Pipeline & Historical Individualization: From Geometric Placeholders to Living Citizens

### The "Geometric Cylinder" Placeholder Failure Mode
In the baseline simulation, the 1,500 simulated citizens were represented by rudimentary geometric primitives (`THREE.CylinderGeometry` for the body, `THREE.SphereGeometry` for the head, and `THREE.ConeGeometry` for the hat). 
- **Visual Disconnect**: While the city architecture had evolved into high-fidelity Gothic brick, timber framing, and authentic outdoor craft stations, the citizens remained untextured primitive cones and cylinders.
- **Lost Economic Atmosphere**: Guild workers—such as the baker with flour apron and bread peel, the carpenter with block plane and felling axe, or the tanner with rawhide apron and fleshing knife—were indistinguishable from guards or patricians except for a basic color tint on their cylinder body.
- **Static Heading**: Citizens walked along streets without facing their movement heading, sliding laterally or backwards through market alleys.

### The Unified Turnaround & Procedural Bmesh Solution

1. **Orthographic 3-View Turnaround Sheets on Unified 16:9 Canvases**:
   - Rather than generating disconnected individual angles (which causes outfit drift and proportions hallucination), a single 16:9 prompt produces Front, Side Profile, and Rear views side-by-side on neutral beige parchment backgrounds.
   - Captured 14 distinct medieval archetypes:
     - `char_baker`: Flour-dusted apron, linen barett cap, 1.8m bread peel shovel, crusty sourdough loaf.
     - `char_tanner`: Dark water-resistant oiled rawhide apron, tied linen coif, high water boots, curved 2-handed iron fleshing knife.
     - `char_carpenter`: Canvas work smock, leather tool belt with iron square and mallet, wooden block plane, broad carpenter felling axe.
     - `char_blacksmith`: Cowhide smithing apron, tied coif, heat-resistant gauntlets, cross-peen hammer & tongs with hot orange billet.
     - `char_brewer`: Brick-red cotte, stained bib apron, chaperon with liripipe, polished copper mash paddle & wooden tasting tankard.
     - `char_potter`: Clay-spattered tunic, cloth headband, bare forearms, wooden shaping rib & terracotta amphora.
     - `char_weaver`: Mi-parti madder red / indigo blue cotte, liripipe hood, threaded hardwood shuttle & tailor shears.
     - `char_stonemason`: White linen coif, stone-dusted smock, split leather apron, iron chisel & round beechwood mallet.
     - `char_woodcutter`: Dark charcoal hooded cowl, forester gauntlets, felling wedges at belt, heavy broad felling axe.
     - `char_friar`: Dominican white habit, dark brown scapular, knotted rope cincture, wooden garden hoe & wicker herb basket with sage/lavender.
     - `char_guard`: Steel kettle hat helmet, chainmail coif, dark red heraldic tabard with golden gate crest, 2.2m steel polearm halberd & arming sword.
     - `char_merchant`: Floor-length royal blue houppelande with beaver fur trim, padded chaperon with cockade, poulaines turnshoes, wax-sealed charter scroll.
     - `char_farmer`: Wide-brimmed straw hat, loose linen shirt, rope belt, curved harvest scythe with bent wooden snath.
     - `char_townswoman`: Forest green kirtle gown, white linen front apron, folded wimple & veil, keys at belt, wicker market basket with bread and root vegetables.

2. **Headless Blender Procedural Construction (`build_characters.py`)**:
   - Created reusable bmesh geometry macro builders (`build_humanoid_base`, `add_apron`, `add_coif_or_hood`, `add_cylinder`, `add_oriented_cylinder`).
   - Standardized on $1.75\text{m}$ overall scale and a $1:6.5$ RTS readability ratio.
   - Ultra-low polygon budget: $140 - 200$ polygons ($280 - 450$ triangles) per character model ($\sim 25\text{ KB}$ per `.glb`).
   - Single-draw-call GLB export directly into `public/models/characters/` and `public/models/`.

3. **Engine Dynamic Dispatch & Deterministic Anti-Cloning Engine**:
   - In `src/engine/three-renderer.ts`, `getCitizenArchetype()` maps citizen `trade`, `guildRank`, and `wealthClass` to the matching 3D character GLB asset.
   - **Deterministic Seeded Jitter**: Using `hashString(c.id)`, individual citizens receive subtle stature scaling jitter:
     $$\text{heightScale} = 1.0 + ((\text{seed} \bmod 13) - 6) \times 0.012 \quad (0.93\times - 1.07\times)$$
     $$\text{girthScale} = 1.0 + (((\text{seed} \times 7) \bmod 13) - 6) \times 0.012 \quad (0.93\times - 1.07\times)$$
     This ensures multiple bakers, carpenters, or guards do not look like carbon clones while preserving historical realism.
   - **Dynamic Locomotion & Heading**:
     - Moving citizens calculate target angle $\theta = \text{atan2}(dx, dz)$ and smoothly rotate toward their heading:
       $$\Delta\theta = \text{normalizeAngle}(\theta_{\text{target}} - \theta_{\text{current}})$$
       $$\theta_{\text{current}} += \Delta\theta \cdot \min(1.0, dt \times 12.0)$$
     - Synchronized walking bob: $Y_{\text{bob}} = |\sin(\text{walkCycle})| \times 0.07\text{m}$.
     - Stride sway: $R_Z = \sin(\text{walkCycle}) \times 0.035\text{ rad}$.
     - Idle workstation breathing: stationary craftspeople at workshops exhibit subtle breathing/working oscillations based on their individual seed offset.

## 16. Texture Pipeline & Lighting: From Flat Colours to Painted Materials

**Context**: Every material was a flat Principled-BSDF colour with no UVs; the city read as pastel boxes with no visible shadows. Full plan, board and before/after: [`docs/material_board/`](docs/material_board/index.html), [`docs/texture_pilot/`](docs/texture_pilot/index.html).

1. **Pipeline** (`scripts/textures/build_textures.ps1` rebuilds `public/textures/` in ~90 s):
   - 7 hero textures are image-generated from concept-art crops + prompts (originals in git-ignored `texture_sources/`), then delit, mean-matched to the style-guide palette, seam-repaired, and given derived normal maps. 12 are procedural (numpy, FFT-based, tileable by construction).
   - `public/textures/manifest.json` records each family's real-world tile size in metres.
2. **UVs are in metres** (`add_world_uvs` in `build_assets.py`): walls project with v = world up, roofs with v = up-slope (ridge at image top), horizontal faces planar X/Y, `TimberDark` along each part's longest edge. Runtime sets `texture.repeat = 1 / tile_m`, so a brick course is 10 cm on every building.
3. **Textures bind by material name** (`src/engine/texture-library.ts` `BINDINGS`). New flat-colour materials keep working; to texture one, add a binding. Shared semantic materials were split per model at export (`MODEL_MATERIAL_OVERRIDES`): `Limestone -> StoneFieldstone` on vernacular plinths, `TimberDark -> TimberLog` on the log-house body.
4. **Pitfalls found (all verified in-engine)**:
   - **Sun behind the camera**: the key light sat at a fixed world offset ~8° from the camera direction, so every visible face was lit alike and every shadow fell behind its object. The sun is now locked to the view (45° up, from screen-left). Shade/lit went 0.75 -> 0.52 (style guide: 0.45–0.55).
   - **Normal-map green is inverted** for these glTF exports (Blender writes v' = 1 - v): `normalScale.y = -1`, otherwise bricks read as lit from below.
   - **AI "seamless" textures tiled left-right but not top-bottom** (5 of 7): measure the wrap edge against the image's own internal edges (`seam_rank`) instead of trusting thumbnails; repair with a best-row crop or a whole-course offset blend.
   - **Tone mapping**: ACES desaturated the painted textures; Neutral's black-level offset crushed the blue channel of dark brick. The rig is balanced so lit walls show textures near their painted value, with `LinearToneMapping`.
   - **Stretched instances smear metre-based UVs**: curtain walls were one 16 m module scaled up to ~4x per segment; they are now tiled 16 m modules.
   - **Inward-wound roofs ("cream plaster blocks")**: `add_pantile_roof` wound every tile course facing down/inward, so baker, brewer, potter, tanner, weaver, blacksmith and watermill showed their plaster upper storey through a missing roof. Fixed in the helper. Replacement runtime materials must also copy `side` from the glTF material (Blender exports `doubleSided`; three.js defaults to `FrontSide`). Audit winding with a per-material up/down triangle count, never by eye.
